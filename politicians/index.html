How Matches Work

The TOP 50 Engine checks each team against 300+ well-known American political figures and judges, past and present, and keeps the strongest clue:

  1. Number. Teams 1 through 47 are matched with that numbered President. Certain famous numbers and years (1600, 1776, 1969...) have their own match.
  2. Name. A politician's name appears in the team name.
  3. Theme. A word in the team name matches something the politician is known for. If several do, the least common word wins.
  4. Hometown. The team's city shares a name with a politician.
  5. The full reading. Otherwise, every candidate is scored on:
    • longevity: seasons in FRC vs. years in public life
    • record: winning teams get famous winners
    • strength: the country's strongest teams get Presidents, Chief Justices and Speakers
    • momentum: teams on the rise get rising stars; veteran teams get elder statesmen
    • game style: auto specialists get planners, endgame specialists get closers, teleop workhorses get workhorses
    • rookie year: the year a team was founded vs. the year someone took office
    • hometown: big-city teams get big-city figures; small-town teams get small-town figures
    • home state
    The best total wins.

Matches are for fun and say nothing about anyone's politics. Each team also gets its real elected officials, listed separately under Your Officials.

About Your Officials

House districts are found from the team's ZIP code (or city, when no ZIP is listed), not its street address. When a ZIP crosses district lines, every district is listed with its share of the ZIP. Nine states (AL, CA, FL, LA, NC, OH, TN, TX, UT) use new district maps starting January 3, 2027.
